A Telegram sponsored message has up to 160 characters and a button. There is no image to carry the message, so every word has to work.

## Start with one clear promise

Pick the single most useful thing your product does for this audience and lead with it. "Launch your online store this weekend" is stronger than "The best e-commerce platform for everyone", because it is specific and easy to picture.

## Use the 3-part structure

Most strong sponsored messages follow the same pattern:

1. **The promise**: what the reader gets.
2. **The proof or detail**: why it is believable or what makes it different.
3. **The nudge**: why to act now.

Example: *"Launch your online store this weekend. Templates, payments and hosting in one place. First month free."* That is about 105 characters, which leaves room to adjust.

## Write for the channel, not for your brand

Your ad appears below posts in channels about a specific topic. Mention the reader's world: a crypto audience, students preparing for exams, small business owners. Ads that match the channel's topic feel relevant and get more clicks.

## Avoid what gets rejected

Telegram's moderation rejects ads with misleading claims, excessive capital letters, emojis used as decoration, and unclear destinations. Keep the tone factual, make sure the landing page matches the promise, and check that your offer is allowed under Telegram's ad policies.

## Always test several angles

Write at least three versions with different angles, for example benefit, urgency and social proof. Run them at the same time with the same targeting and keep the winner. Small wording changes often make a big difference.
