A big subscriber count does not mean a big audience. Before you buy a post, spend ten minutes on these checks. They will save you from most bad deals.

## 1. Compare views to subscribers

Open the channel's last 10 to 20 posts and look at the view counts. Divide the typical views by the subscriber count. A healthy channel usually shows a steady share of its subscribers reading each post. If a channel has hundreds of thousands of subscribers but only a few hundred views per post, most of those subscribers are not real readers.

## 2. Look for steady, natural view counts

Real audiences produce views that vary from post to post. If every post has almost exactly the same number of views, or views jump suddenly on one post, be careful: views may have been bought.

## 3. Check the posting rhythm

How often does the channel post, and how many of those posts are ads? A channel that publishes ten ads a day trains its readers to scroll past them. Look for a balance between useful content and advertising.

## 4. Read the comments and reactions

If the channel has comments or reactions enabled, read them. Real discussions, questions and varied reactions are good signs. Empty comment sections on a "huge" channel are not.

## 5. Check the audience fit

Read a week of posts. Would your customer find this channel useful? Language, region and topic matter more than size. A smaller channel that matches your buyer often beats a big general one.

## 6. Ask for recent results

Ask the owner what other advertisers achieved: clicks, joins or sales. Good channel owners keep this data and share it. Vague answers are a warning sign.

## 7. Work out the real price

Divide the price of the post by the views you can realistically expect. Compare that cost per thousand views across your shortlist instead of comparing prices alone.
